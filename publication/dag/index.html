Questions and answers

Who created QuoteLedger?

QuoteLedger is attributed to Aaron Michael Weis (ΩNeiron™), author-supplied ORCID 0009-0002-1906-148X. The release discloses AI assistance in implementation, testing and writing.

What problem does QuoteLedger address?

It keeps an imported research lead distinct from an exact retained quotation, a source declared primary and full text, and a current review record.

Can QuoteLedger verify that a cited paper exists?

No. It does not query publishers, search engines, DOI registries or other external services. Source identity and URLs remain supplied assertions.

Does a matching quotation prove the claim?

No. A literal quote may be irrelevant or used to support an unjustified generalization. The false-but-consistent example makes this limitation explicit.

Can an abstract count as primary full-text evidence?

An exact abstract quote can reach local_quote. It does not reach primary_quote under an honest abstract extent label. The software cannot detect a falsely supplied full_text label.

Can I bring a research-tool or Elicit CSV into QuoteLedger?

You can map a CSV with claim, title and URL columns using configurable headers. The original bytes and extra columns are preserved. No native Elicit API integration or exhaustive export-format compatibility is claimed.

What changes make a review stale?

Changing claim text, citations, declared basis or cited source metadata changes the claim binding. Review fields themselves are editable and are not authenticated.

What does primary_reviewed mean?

It means the record has a matching quote from a source declared primary/full text and a current completed supports review. It does not mean the source, reviewer or proposition has been authenticated.

Does the tool send private research to a model?

The supplied tool performs no model or network calls. It reads local declared files when asked. Imported CSV records may still contain private information and must be reviewed before sharing.

How are quotation offsets counted?

Offsets are half-open Unicode code-point positions in strict UTF-8 decoded text. They are not byte offsets or PDF positions. Unicode spelling and line endings are not normalized.

What was actually tested for version 0.1.0?

The included local receipts record 67 passing unit tests, 20 authored cases and 64 generated checks matching expectations, and ten packaging checks. The checks overlap and are not a citation-quality study.

Where are the human article and dense PROSE companion?

The readable article is publication/index.html in the release. Questions, answers, the glossary, explicit PROSE and typed graph are under publication/dag/. They describe the same supplied work record.

How should QuoteLedger be cited?

Cite Aaron Michael Weis, QuoteLedger, version 0.1.0, using the included CFF, BibTeX or plain citation. The accompanying paper is A Citation Is Not a Check. No repository URL or DOI has been assigned in this candidate.

Is QuoteLedger a general anti-hallucination solution?

No. It is a bounded local record inspector. It can expose missing evidence conditions, changed snapshots and stale bindings, but it cannot determine semantic truth or discover claims omitted from the packet.

human_intro: "A reference at the end of a sentence can make unfinished research look finished. The paper might be real. The quoted sentence might be real too. Neither fact tells us whether someone opened the right version, read beyond the abstract or checked that the passage supports the conclusion.\n\nQuoteLedger gives that unfinished work a place to live. A research lead stays a lead until its record contains more. You can retain a text snapshot, locate the passage you intend to quote and check that the recorded bytes and exact passage still match. If a human reviews the claim, that judgment is tied to the claim and source records they inspected.\n\nImagine a fictional report that says eight specimens took twelve minutes in one trial. Turning that into “the method always finishes every job in twelve minutes” changes the meaning. QuoteLedger can show that the quotation is intact and the review is current. It cannot make that leap in meaning valid. The package includes this deliberately wrong example so that a successful check is never confused with proof.\n\nThe useful distinction is between a link, an exact local quotation, a source declared primary and full text, and a completed review. Those are different records, not increasing confidence scores. An abstract can remain useful without being mislabeled as a full-text check. A changed claim can keep its source while losing the applicability of an old review.\n\nThe small Python tool runs locally without a model or network connection. Its CSV intake preserves original bytes and extra columns rather than throwing away context. It can prepare pending review records and produce JSON or a readable report. It does not rewrite the original packet, fetch papers or approve research on your behalf.\n\nThe accompanying working paper explains the contract and its limits. The recorded release passed 67 unit tests, matched 20 authored cases and 64 generated checks, and passed ten packaging checks. These are synthetic implementation checks, not a measurement of better real-world citations.\n\nThe goal is modest and practical: make it easier to see what has actually been checked and what still deserves a person's attention. The structured companion keeps the detailed records available without making this introduction read like a database."
